India’s Urban Flooding Crisis — Lessons from 2024 Disasters

The Hindu | Date: 2024 • ~600 words

Introduction

In 2024, several Indian cities — including Delhi, Chennai, and Bengaluru — witnessed severe urban flooding, causing loss of lives, property damage, and disruption of economic activities. The Hindu emphasized that these disasters are not merely natural but a result of poor urban planning, encroachment, and climate change. 1. Restore Natural Ecosystems: Protect wetlands, river floodplains, and urban lakes.

Conclusion

Urban flooding is no longer a one-off event but a systemic crisis aggravated by poor planning and climate change. Preventing it requires a shift from reactive relief to proactive resilience-building, blending ecological restoration with governance reforms. For UPSC aspirants, the issue exemplifies the intersection of environment, governance, and urbanization in India’s developmental challenges.
