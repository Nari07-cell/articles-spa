India’s Urban Waste Management — From Swachh Bharat to Circular Economy

The Hindu | Date: 2024 • ~600 words

Introduction

India’s rapid urbanization has created a mounting waste management crisis. Cities generate over 160,000 tonnes of municipal solid waste daily, of which only ~75% is collected and ~25% scientifically processed. In 2024, The Hindu highlighted how India must move beyond Swachh Bharat’s cleanliness drive toward a circular economy approach. ________________________________________ Current Scenario • Solid Waste: ~62 million tonnes annually; expected to reach 165 million by 2030. • Plastic Waste: ~3.5 million tonnes annually, rising sharply with e-commerce. • E-Waste: India among world’s top 5 producers (~1 million tonnes annually). • Hazardous Waste: Poor segregation and treatment capacity. ________________________________________ Government Initiatives 1. Swachh Bharat Mission (SBM 2.0, 2021–26): Focus on source segregation, scientific processing. 2. Plastic Waste Management Rules (2016, amended 2022): Single-use plastic ban. 3. E-Waste Management Rules (2016, amended 2022): Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR). 4. Waste-to-Energy Plants: Several operational but facing efficiency and emission issues. ________________________________________ Challenges 1. Segregation Gaps: Only ~60% households practice waste segregation. 2. Capacity Constraints: Urban local bodies (ULBs) lack funds and technical expertise. 3. Informal Sector Neglect: Ragpickers and waste workers remain marginalized. 4. Technology Failures: Waste-to-energy plants underperform due to poor feedstock quality. 5. Behavioral Barriers: Public awareness low, compliance weak. ________________________________________ Towards a Circular Economy • Reduce: Ban single-use plastics, encourage eco-friendly packaging. • Reuse: Promote refill stations, shared economy models. • Recycle: Strengthen EPR, integrate informal sector with formal recycling. • Recover: Energy recovery from biodegradable waste through composting, biomethanation. ________________________________________ Case Studies • Indore: Model city with 100% segregation, decentralized composting, and waste-to-energy. • Kerala: Decentralized waste management with community participation. ________________________________________ UPSC Relevance • GS-III (Environment): Solid waste, pollution, circular economy. • GS-II (Governance): Role of ULBs, Swachh Bharat Mission. • Essay: o “Waste to wealth: A pathway to sustainable development.” o “Urbanization and the challenge of environmental governance.” • PSIR Paper II: Policy capacity in environmental governance. ________________________________________

Conclusion

Conclusion

Urban waste management is no longer just a sanitation issue but a climate, health, and governance challenge. India must transition from linear “collect–dump” models to a circular economy emphasizing reduction, recycling, and recovery. For UPSC aspirants, it reflects how urban governance and sustainability intersect in policy practice.
