India’s Water Security — Inter-State River Disputes and Policy Challenges

The Hindu | Date: 2024 • ~600 words

Introduction

Water is emerging as India’s most critical natural resource challenge. The Hindu in 2024 highlighted how recurring inter-state river disputes — Cauvery, Krishna, Ravi-Beas, and Yamuna — reflect governance gaps, competing demands, and ecological stress. Water security is central for sustainable development, federalism, and social stability. ________________________________________

Introduction

Current Status
• Per Capita Availability: Declined from 5,177 m³ (1951) to ~1,500 m³ (2024), approaching scarcity threshold (1,000 m³).
• Disputes Pending: 30+ inter-state water conflicts under tribunals or SC.
• Climate Stress: Erratic monsoons, declining groundwater, glacier retreat.
• Urban–Rural Tensions: Cities like Bengaluru face “Day Zero” threats.
________________________________________
Key Disputes
1. Cauvery: Karnataka–Tamil Nadu–Kerala–Puducherry over irrigation releases.
2. Krishna: Maharashtra–Karnataka–Telangana–Andhra Pradesh disputes despite tribunal awards.
3. Ravi-Beas: Punjab–Haryana conflict; linked to SYL canal politics.
4. Yamuna: Delhi–Haryana disputes over drinking water share.
________________________________________
Drivers of Conflict
• Unequal Rainfall Dependence: Monsoon failures heighten disputes.
• Fragmented Governance: River Boards weak; tribunals slow (decades for awards).
• Growing Demands: Urbanization, agriculture, industry compete.
• Ecological Decline: Pollution, sand mining, deforestation worsen scarcity.
________________________________________
Government Framework
• Inter-State River Water Disputes Act (1956): Tribunals set up, but delayed implementation.
• River Boards Act (1956): Largely unused.
• Draft National Water Policy (2021): Emphasizes demand management, recycling.
• Atal Bhujal Yojana: Groundwater management in stressed states.
________________________________________
Challenges
• Legal–Political Deadlocks: States often defy tribunal/SC rulings.
• Weak Institutions: Lack of empowered River Basin Authorities.
• Climate Variability: Drought–flood cycles intensify disputes.
• Equity Issues: Farmers vs cities; upstream vs downstream.

Way Forward
1. River Basin Authorities: Empowered, technocratic, and multi-state bodies.
2. Water Use Efficiency: Micro-irrigation, crop diversification, urban recycling.
3. Legal Reform: Strengthen timelines and enforceability of tribunal awards.
4. Data Transparency: Common hydrological databases.
5. Community Approaches: Participatory water governance (pani panchayats, watershed models).
________________________________________
Conclusion

Conclusion

India’s water security challenge is as much about governance and cooperation as about natural scarcity. Without institutional reforms and cooperative federalism, disputes risk undermining development and social harmony. For UPSC aspirants, this theme illustrates how environment, federalism, and governance converge in India’s policy landscape.
