India’s Urbanisation Challenge — Infrastructure, Governance, and Sustainability

Indian Express | Date: 2024 • ~600 words

Introduction

India is undergoing a rapid urban transition, with cities projected to house 50% of the population by 2050. In 2024, Indian Express highlighted growing concerns around infrastructure stress, inequality, and environmental degradation in Indian cities. For UPSC, urbanisation is a key theme linking economy, governance, and sustainability. ________________________________________

Introduction

Current Status
• Urban Population (2024): ~36%.
• Slum Dwellers: ~65 million people.
• Infrastructure Deficit: Public transport share <30%, inadequate water supply in Tier-2/3 cities.
• Pollution: 14 of the world’s 20 most polluted cities in India.
• Smart Cities Mission: 100 cities covered, with mixed outcomes.
________________________________________
Key Challenges
1. Housing Shortages: 3 crore+ urban housing deficit.
2. Transport Crisis: Congestion, weak public transit, pollution.
3. Water & Sanitation: Many cities face supply shortages and sewage mismanagement.
4. Informal Settlements: Slums lacking tenure rights and basic amenities.
5. Governance Gaps: ULBs underfunded and lack autonomy.
________________________________________
Government Measures
• Smart Cities Mission (2015): ICT-driven urban improvements.
• AMRUT (2015): Urban water supply, sewerage upgrades.
• PM Awas Yojana (Urban): Affordable housing push.
• Metro Expansion: 20+ cities with operational metro rail.
• National Urban Policy Framework (2018): Guidance for city planning.
________________________________________
Achievements
• Metro Success Stories: Delhi, Bengaluru improving connectivity.
• Digital Urban Governance: GIS mapping, online grievance redressal in some cities.
• Affordable Housing: Millions of homes sanctioned under PMAY-U.
• Awareness: Citizens more engaged in city development debates.
________________________________________
Persistent Gaps
• Uneven Implementation: Smart Cities outcomes vary widely.
• ULB Weakness: Municipalities lack staff, funds, and planning powers.
• Environmental Stress: Air, water, and waste challenges intensify.
• Equity Issues: Urban poor often excluded from planning.
________________________________________
Significance
• Economic Growth: Cities contribute ~70% of GDP.
• Social Justice: Inclusive cities crucial for equity.
• Environment: Urban design affects sustainability and resilience.
• Governance: ULB empowerment key to democracy.
________________________________________
UPSC Relevance
• GS-I (Urbanisation): Issues and remedies.
• GS-II (Governance): Local bodies, decentralisation.
• GS-III (Environment & Economy): Urban sustainability.
• Essay:
o “India’s future is urban, but will it be sustainable?”
o “Smart cities require smart governance, not just smart technology.”
• PSIR Paper II: Urban governance and public policy.
________________________________________
Way Forward
1. Strengthen ULBs: Financial devolution and technical capacity.
2. Affordable Housing: Expand rental and slum upgrading models.
3. Sustainable Transport: Buses, metro, cycling infrastructure.
4. Green Cities: Blue–green infrastructure for resilience.
5. Citizen Participation: Participatory planning and accountability.
________________________________________
Conclusion

Conclusion

India’s urbanisation presents both opportunities and risks. Sustainable and inclusive cities require empowered ULBs, robust infrastructure, and citizen engagement. For UPSC aspirants, this topic captures the intersection of governance, economy, and sustainability.
