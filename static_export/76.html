India’s Urban Housing Challenge — Affordable Housing and Policy Gaps

Indian Express | Date: 2024 • ~600 words

Introduction

Housing is a basic human need, yet India faces a massive urban housing shortage, particularly for the poor. In 2024, Indian Express highlighted that despite ambitious housing schemes, affordability and access remain critical challenges. Introduction

Current Status
• Urban Housing Shortage (MoHUA, 2023): ~3 crore units, 95% in EWS/LIG categories.
• PM Awas Yojana (Urban): Target 1.12 crore houses; ~75 lakh completed by 2024.
• Urbanisation: ~36% population urban in 2024; projected 50% by 2050.
• Slums: ~65 million people still live in slums.
• Real Estate Prices: Rising far faster than income growth in cities.
________________________________________
Key Challenges
1. Affordability Gap: Low-income households priced out of formal housing.
2. Land Scarcity: Rising land prices in urban centres.
3. Slow Implementation: Delays in housing projects, cost overruns.
4. Financing Barriers: Poor access to credit for informal workers.
5. Rental Market Weakness: Lack of affordable, formal rental housing.
________________________________________
Government Initiatives
• PMAY (Urban): Subsidised housing through CLSS and in-situ redevelopment.
• RERA (2016): Increased transparency in real estate.
• Smart Cities Mission: Incorporates housing with urban planning.
• Affordable Rental Housing Complexes (ARHC, 2020): For migrant workers.
• State Programs: Delhi’s DDA housing, Mumbai’s SRA redevelopment.
________________________________________
Achievements
• Scale of Construction: Millions of houses sanctioned and built.
• Technology Use: Prefabricated and green building solutions emerging.
• Increased Awareness: Housing seen as core part of urban policy.
________________________________________
Persistent Gaps
• Targeting Issues: Many EWS/LIG families still excluded.
• Quality Concerns: Poor construction and amenities in some projects.
• Displacement Risks: Redevelopment displaces slum dwellers.
• Coordination Failures: Centre–state–ULB alignment weak.
________________________________________
Significance
• Social Justice: Affordable housing reduces inequality and vulnerability.
• Economic Growth: Real estate and construction generate jobs.
• Urban Sustainability: Planned housing reduces slum proliferation.
• Gender Empowerment: Housing titles in women’s names improve security.
________________________________________
UPSC Relevance
• GS-I (Society): Urbanisation and social issues.
• GS-II (Governance): Welfare delivery and housing schemes.
• GS-III (Economy): Real estate and infrastructure.
• Essay:
o “Housing is the foundation of dignity and equality.”
o “Urbanisation without affordable housing is a recipe for crisis.”
• PSIR Paper II: Rights and governance in urban policy.
________________________________________
Way Forward
1. Expand Affordable Rentals: Strengthen ARHC with PPP models.
2. Improve Financing: Microfinance and credit access for informal workers.
3. Land Reforms: Unlock land banks, promote vertical housing.
4. Strengthen Implementation: Transparent, timely delivery of projects.
5. Sustainable Housing: Green materials, energy-efficient designs.
________________________________________
Conclusion

Conclusion

India’s urban housing challenge is not just about building homes, but about creating inclusive, sustainable cities. Ensuring affordable housing for all requires synergy between policy, markets, and community participation. For UPSC aspirants, it demonstrates the intersection of social justice, governance, and urbanisation.
