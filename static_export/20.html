India’s Food Security Challenges Amid Climate Change

The Hindu | Date: 2024 • ~600 words

Introduction

Food security in India — defined by availability, access, utilization, and stability — faces new threats due to climate change and global disruptions. The Hindu in 2024 highlighted how erratic monsoons, rising temperatures, and supply chain shocks are straining India’s ability to ensure affordable and nutritious food. Way Forward
1. Diversify Cropping Patterns: Encourage millets, pulses, and oilseeds.
2. Invest in R&D: Climate-resilient seed varieties and precision farming.
3. Strengthen Nutrition Programs: Midday Meal, ICDS to focus on proteins and micronutrients.
4. Empower Farmers: Access to credit, insurance, and market linkages.
5. Water Management: Promote drip irrigation, rainwater harvesting, and crop zoning.

Conclusion

Conclusion

India’s food security rests on balancing quantity with quality in an era of climate shocks. The challenge lies not only in producing enough but in ensuring diverse, nutritious, and sustainable food systems. For UPSC aspirants, this theme bridges social justice, agriculture, climate change, and governance — making it central for GS-II, GS-III, and Essay papers.
