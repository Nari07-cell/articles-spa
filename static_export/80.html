Introduction

Current Status
• Vehicle Growth: 30+ crore registered vehicles in India (MoRTH, 2024).
• Congestion Costs: Delhi, Mumbai among most congested cities globally (TomTom Index).
• Public Transport Share: <30% in most metros (vs global average ~60%).
• Metro Rail: 900+ km operational across 20+ cities.
• Pollution Link: Transport contributes ~30% of urban air pollution.
________________________________________
Key Challenges
1. Congestion: Poor road planning, rising private vehicle ownership.
2. Weak Public Transport: Overcrowded buses, limited coverage.
3. Last-Mile Gaps: Inadequate pedestrian and cycling infrastructure.
4. Pollution & Emissions: Vehicle emissions driving poor AQI.
5. Institutional Fragmentation: Multiple agencies, weak coordination.
________________________________________
Government Initiatives
• Metro Rail Expansion: Rapid build-out in Tier-1 and Tier-2 cities.
• FAME-II Scheme: Incentives for EVs and charging infrastructure.
• Urban Transport Policy (2006, revised): Emphasis on sustainable mobility.
• Smart Cities Mission: Promoting integrated mobility.
• PM Gati Shakti Plan: Multimodal connectivity framework.
________________________________________
Achievements
• Metro Success Stories: Delhi, Bengaluru, Mumbai improving urban mobility.
• EV Growth: 1.5 million EVs sold in 2023–24.
• App-Based Services: Ride-hailing and shared mobility expanding.
• Awareness: Citizens increasingly conscious of sustainability.
________________________________________
Persistent Gaps
• Metro Affordability: High fares deter daily wage earners.
• Bus Decline: Fleet size shrinking in many cities.
• Land Use–Transport Disconnect: Poor integration with urban planning.
• Safety Concerns: Pedestrian deaths, unsafe cycling.
• Equity Issues: Poor, women, elderly face mobility challenges.
________________________________________
Significance
• Economic Growth: Efficient transport boosts productivity.
• Environment: Sustainable transport cuts emissions and pollution.
• Social Justice: Affordable mobility key to inclusion.
• Urban Planning: Mobility central to sustainable cities.
________________________________________
UPSC Relevance
• GS-I (Urbanisation): Infrastructure and challenges.
• GS-II (Governance): Role of ULBs, service delivery.
• GS-III (Environment & Economy): Pollution, sustainable development.

o “Mobility is the backbone of urban life.”

Way Forward
1. Bus System Revival: Expand fleets, adopt electric buses.
2. Affordable Metro: Fare rationalisation and subsidies.
3. Non-Motorised Transport: Pedestrian zones, cycling tracks.
4. Integrated Planning: Unified transport authorities in cities.
5. Green Mobility: Promote EVs, CNG, hydrogen fuel.
________________________________________
Conclusion

Conclusion

India’s urban transport is at a crossroads — balancing growth with sustainability. A people-centric, integrated approach is key to ensuring mobility as a right for all. For UPSC aspirants, it demonstrates how infrastructure, governance, and social justice converge in urban policy.
