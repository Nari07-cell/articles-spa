How India’s and Pakistan’s Armed Forces Compare, as Nuclear Fears Grow

Newsweek | Date: Apr 30, 2025 • ~1300 words

Introduction

South Asia remains one of the most volatile regions of the world. The April 2025 Newsweek feature on India–Pakistan military balance highlighted rising nuclear fears in a context of historical animosity, asymmetrical military power, and fragile deterrence. India and Pakistan have fought four wars and continue to experience regular border tensions. Both nations possess nuclear weapons, making the region a flashpoint for global security. India’s Conventional Strengths • Personnel: ~1.4 million active troops; world’s second-largest standing army. • Budget: USD 83 billion (2024), fourth-highest worldwide. • Modernization: Rafale jets, S-400 air defense systems, indigenously developed Tejas, and missile programs (Agni, Prithvi). • Navy: INS Vikrant & INS Vikramaditya (aircraft carriers), nuclear-powered submarines. • Space & Cyber: Defence Space Agency and emerging cyber-warfare capabilities. Pakistan’s Strategic Posture • Personnel: ~640,000 active. • Budget: USD 11 billion (2024), but 3.6% of GDP (high relative burden). • Doctrine: Emphasis on nuclear deterrence and asymmetric warfare. • Arms Dependency: Reliance on Chinese JF-17 fighters, Turkish drones, US legacy equipment. • Proxy Strategy: Use of militant groups to offset India’s conventional edge. Nuclear Dynamics • India: ~164 warheads, “No First Use” doctrine. • Pakistan: ~170 warheads, “First Use” policy if survival threatened. • Delivery Systems: India’s long-range Agni missiles vs Pakistan’s short-range Nasr tactical nukes. Escalation Risks • Miscalculation at LoC. • Cross-border terrorism leading to escalation (e.g., Pulwama 2019 precedent). • Tactical nukes destabilizing deterrence framework. Global Implications • China: Supports Pakistan to tie down India. • US & Russia: Call for restraint, wary of nuclear flashpoint. • UN & Multilateralism: Limited leverage in South Asia’s bilateral hostility. UPSC Relevance • GS-II: Neighborhood relations, international security. • GS-III: Internal and external security challenges. • PSIR: Deterrence theory, balance of power, South Asian security complex.
