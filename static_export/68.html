India’s Energy Security — Balancing Fossil Fuels and Transition Goals

Economic Times | Date: 2024 • ~600 words

Introduction

India is the third-largest energy consumer globally, with demand projected to double by 2040. In 2024, Economic Times highlighted the twin challenge of ensuring affordable energy for growth while meeting climate transition commitments. For UPSC, energy security reflects the convergence of economy, environment, and geopolitics. ________________________________________ Current Energy Mix (2024) • Coal: ~55% of installed capacity; still backbone of electricity. • Renewables: ~180 GW capacity (solar, wind, hydro, biomass). • Oil Imports: ~85% dependent on imports. • Natural Gas: ~6% of primary energy; target 15% by 2030. • Nuclear: 7,000+ MW operational, modest share. ________________________________________ Key Challenges 1. Import Dependence: Oil import bill ~$160 billion (2023–24). 2. Coal Dilemma: Needed for baseload power, but high emissions. 3. Renewable Integration: Intermittency and grid management issues. 4. Geopolitical Risks: West Asia tensions, Russia–Ukraine war, supply disruptions. 5. Financial Stress: DISCOM losses, subsidy burdens. ________________________________________ Government Measures • Integrated Energy Policy: Diversification and efficiency. • National Hydrogen Mission (2023): Focus on green hydrogen as fuel. • Ethanol Blending: 12% achieved in 2023–24, target 20% by 2025. • Strategic Oil Reserves: Capacity ~9.5 MMT (10 days of imports). • PLI Schemes: Solar modules, battery storage, EV manufacturing. • International Engagements: ISA, IEA, and bilateral oil deals. ________________________________________ Opportunities • Renewable Leadership: Potential to export solar and hydrogen tech. • Domestic Gas Expansion: New exploration blocks, LNG terminals. • Energy Efficiency: PAT scheme, UJALA LED, EV adoption. • Regional Cooperation: Energy trade with Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh. • Innovation: Offshore wind, CCUS (Carbon Capture, Utilisation & Storage). ________________________________________ Significance • Economic Growth: Energy drives manufacturing and services. • Strategic Autonomy: Reducing import dependence enhances sovereignty. • Climate Goals: Essential for net-zero by 2070. • Social Equity: Affordable energy central to welfare. ________________________________________ UPSC Relevance • GS-III (Economy & Environment): Energy policy, renewables. • GS-II (IR): Energy diplomacy. • Essay: o “Energy security is the foundation of national security.” o “India’s growth story is an energy story.” • PSIR Paper II: Resource politics and strategic autonomy. ________________________________________
