India’s Infrastructure Push — Growth Driver or Fiscal Strain?

Economic Times | Date: 2024 • ~600 words

Introduction

Infrastructure is the backbone of India’s economic growth strategy. In 2024, Economic Times discussed the government’s massive investment in roads, railways, and energy under the National Infrastructure Pipeline (NIP) and Gati Shakti Mission, while raising concerns over fiscal strain. Introduction

Current Status
• National Infrastructure Pipeline (NIP): ₹111 lakh crore investment plan (2020–25).
• Budget 2024–25 Capex: ₹11.11 lakh crore (~3.4% of GDP).
• Key Sectors: Roads (28%), energy (24%), urban infra (17%), railways (12%).
• Logistics Cost: 13–14% of GDP vs 8–9% in advanced economies.
• Private Participation: Lagging due to weak PPP models.
________________________________________
Achievements
1. Road Expansion: NH length >1.45 lakh km; Bharatmala in progress.
2. Railway Modernisation: Electrification of 90% routes; Vande Bharat trains launched.
3. Port Efficiency: Sagarmala projects improving cargo handling.
4. Urban Infra: Metro expansion in 20+ cities.
5. Digital Infra: BharatNet fibre reaching rural areas.
________________________________________
Key Challenges
1. Financing Stress: High government borrowing, limited private sector appetite.
2. Project Delays: Land acquisition, clearances, and litigation.
3. PPP Fatigue: Post-2010 slowdown in private infra investment.
4. Logistics Inefficiency: Poor multimodal integration.
5. Regional Disparities: Eastern and NE states lagging behind.
________________________________________
Government Measures
• PM Gati Shakti (2021): Integrated infrastructure masterplan.
• NIP (2020): Long-term infrastructure pipeline.
• InvITs & REITs: Mobilising private capital.
• National Monetisation Pipeline (2021): Asset monetisation for funding.
• Green Infra Push: Solar parks, EV charging networks.
________________________________________
Significance
• Economic Growth: Infrastructure multiplier effect on jobs and industry.
• Global Competitiveness: Reduces logistics cost, boosts exports.
• Social Inclusion: Improves access to health, education, connectivity.
• Sustainability: Green infra critical for climate goals.
________________________________________
UPSC Relevance
• GS-III (Economy): Infrastructure, investment.
• GS-II (Governance): Centre–state cooperation, PPPs.
• Essay:
o “Infrastructure is not just steel and concrete, but the foundation of inclusive growth.”
o “India’s infrastructure challenge is as much about governance as finance.”
• PSIR Paper II: Political economy of development.
________________________________________
Way Forward
1. Mobilise Private Capital: Strengthen PPP models, reduce risks.
2. Efficient Project Management: Land pooling, faster clearances.
3. Regional Balance: Prioritise backward regions in allocation.
4. Sustainable Financing: Green bonds, infrastructure funds.
5. Multimodal Integration: Road–rail–port–air synergies under Gati Shakti.
________________________________________
Conclusion

Conclusion

India’s infrastructure push is ambitious and necessary, but fiscal sustainability and private sector revival are crucial for long-term success. For UPSC aspirants, this theme shows how economy, governance, and sustainability converge in development policy.
