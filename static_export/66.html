India’s Unemployment Challenge — Beyond Jobless Growth

Indian Express | Date: 2024 • ~600 words

Introduction

Unemployment remains a persistent structural challenge for India, despite being one of the fastest-growing economies. In 2024, Indian Express analysed how job creation has lagged behind GDP growth, sparking debates over “jobless growth.” For UPSC, this issue represents the intersection of economy, social justice, and governance. ________________________________________

Introduction

Current Status
• Unemployment Rate (CMIE 2024): ~7%.
• Youth Unemployment: ~23% among 15–29 age group.
• Sectoral Employment: Agriculture still employs ~45%, industry ~25%, services ~30%.
• Labour Force Participation Rate (LFPR): ~40%, among the lowest globally.
• Gender Gap: Women’s workforce participation ~24%.
________________________________________
Causes
1. Jobless Growth: Automation and capital-intensive industries dominate.
2. Skill Mismatch: Education not aligned with industry needs.
3. Informality: 80–85% workforce in informal/unorganised sector.
4. Low Female Participation: Social norms, safety issues, care burden.
5. Regional Disparities: Northern states show higher unemployment.
________________________________________
Government Initiatives
• Skill India Mission (2015): Target 400 million skilled workers.
• Make in India: Promote manufacturing-led job creation.
• Atmanirbhar Bharat Package: MSME credit, PLI schemes.
• MGNREGA: Social security and rural employment.
• Startup India: Fostering entrepreneurship and job multipliers.
________________________________________
Challenges
• Slow Formal Job Growth: Most new jobs remain low-paying, informal.
• Automation Threat: AI and robotics disrupting employment.
• Agriculture Distress: Hidden unemployment in farm sector.
• Urban Underemployment: Gig and platform economy with poor protections.
• Policy Coordination: Multiple schemes but fragmented approach.
________________________________________
Significance
• Economic Stability: Jobs central to growth sustainability.
• Social Justice: Employment linked to dignity, empowerment, equality.
• Political Stability: Unemployment is a driver of discontent.
• Demographic Dividend: Without jobs, India risks a demographic disaster.
________________________________________
UPSC Relevance
• GS-III (Economy): Employment, growth, poverty reduction.
• GS-II (Governance): Welfare policies, skill building.
• Essay:
o “Employment is the real measure of economic growth.”
o “India’s demographic dividend may turn into a liability.”
• PSIR Paper II: Political economy of labour.
________________________________________
Way Forward
1. Labour-Intensive Manufacturing: Textiles, food processing, electronics assembly.
2. Boost MSMEs: Credit, tech adoption, and cluster development.
3. Skill Alignment: Industry-academia linkages for employability.
4. Women’s Employment: Childcare, safety, equal pay, flexible work.
5. Green Jobs: Renewable energy, waste management, climate adaptation.
________________________________________
Conclusion

Conclusion

India’s unemployment problem requires structural reforms in industry, skilling, and labour markets. Economic growth must translate into quality, inclusive jobs. For UPSC aspirants, it demonstrates how economics, governance, and society converge in policymaking.
