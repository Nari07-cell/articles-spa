Introduction

Current Status
• FLFPR (2024): ~25% (World Bank), compared to ~60% global average.
• Employment Types: Most women concentrated in informal, unpaid, or agricultural work.
• Urban–Rural Divide: Rural participation slightly higher due to agricultural work, but urban women face cultural and structural barriers.
• Global Rank: India ranked ~127/146 in WEF Global Gender Gap Report (2024).
________________________________________
Causes of Low Participation
1. Cultural Norms: Patriarchal attitudes discourage women from working outside the home.
2. Safety Concerns: Poor urban infrastructure and harassment risks reduce mobility.
3. Skill & Education Mismatch: Women overrepresented in low-skill, underpaid jobs.
4. Care Burden: Unpaid domestic work and caregiving responsibilities limit participation.
5. Informality: Lack of maternity benefits, flexible work, and job security.
________________________________________
Government Initiatives
• Maternity Benefit (Amendment) Act, 2017: Extended paid maternity leave to 26 weeks.
• Beti Bachao Beti Padhao: Focus on education and empowerment.
• Skill India & PMKVY: Special focus on women in non-traditional trades.
• Mahila E-Haat & Stand Up India: Encourage entrepreneurship.
• Reservation in Local Governance: 33% women in Panchayats; 2023 Bill for 33% women in Lok Sabha/Assemblies (awaiting implementation post-delimitation).
________________________________________
Challenges
• Implementation Gaps: Many schemes fail to reach intended beneficiaries.
• Workplace Discrimination: Gender pay gap ~28% (ILO).
• Digital Divide: Women’s internet access lower than men’s.
• Urban Bias: Policies often neglect rural and informal women workers.
________________________________________
Significance
• Economic Growth: McKinsey estimates closing gender gap could add $770 billion to India’s GDP by 2025.
• Social Justice: Women’s participation crucial for inclusive development.
• Democracy: Empowered women workers strengthen political voice and citizenship.

Way Forward
1. Workplace Reforms: Equal pay, flexible hours, safe working conditions.
2. Childcare Infrastructure: Public crèches and affordable daycare.
3. Skill Upgradation: Encourage women in STEM, green jobs, and entrepreneurship.
4. Cultural Change: Awareness campaigns challenging patriarchal norms.
5. Policy Integration: Gender budgeting across ministries for holistic outcomes.

Conclusion

Conclusion

India’s gender gap in work participation is not just a women’s issue but a national development challenge. Closing it requires simultaneous reforms in labour markets, social attitudes, and welfare support. For UPSC aspirants, this issue demonstrates how economy, society, and governance converge in gender equality debates.
