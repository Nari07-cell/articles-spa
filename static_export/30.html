India’s Urban Housing Challenge — Affordable Homes for All

Indian Express | Date: 2024 • ~600 words

Introduction

Housing is a fundamental human need and a marker of social justice. Despite rapid urbanization, India faces a shortage of ~30 million urban housing units, mostly for the economically weaker sections (EWS) and low-income groups (LIG). In 2024, the Indian Express highlighted India’s urban housing challenge, emphasizing gaps in affordability, planning, and governance. ________________________________________ Scale of the Problem • Shortage: Urban housing shortage ~30 million, rural ~20 million. • Slums: Nearly 65 million people live in slums or informal settlements. • Affordability Gap: Housing costs in metros are 7–8 times average annual income. • Demand–Supply Mismatch: Luxury housing oversupplied; EWS/LIG housing undersupplied. ________________________________________ Key Government Initiatives 1. Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana (PMAY-Urban) o Target: Housing for all by 2022 (extended to 2024–25). o ~1.2 crore houses sanctioned; ~75 lakh completed. 2. Affordable Rental Housing Complexes (ARHCs) o Addressing migrant workers’ housing needs. 3. Smart Cities Mission o Integrated housing and infrastructure development. 4. State Schemes o Tamil Nadu’s Tamil Nadu Housing Board, Gujarat’s Mukhyamantri Gruh Yojana, etc. ________________________________________ Drivers of the Housing Crisis • Land Scarcity & Cost: High land prices in urban centers. • Regulatory Bottlenecks: Lengthy approvals, weak enforcement of inclusionary zoning. • Financing Gaps: Limited access to credit for EWS/LIG. • Unplanned Urbanization: Encroachments, informal settlements mushrooming. • Climate Risks: Floods, heatwaves increasingly affect urban housing resilience. ________________________________________ Challenges • Quality of Construction: Many affordable houses are poorly built, leading to underutilization. • Vacant Homes Paradox: ~1.1 crore urban homes remain vacant due to affordability mismatch. • Private Sector Reluctance: Developers focus on high-end housing for profitability. • Social Exclusion: Women, migrants, and marginalized groups face barriers in ownership. ________________________________________ UPSC Relevance • GS-I (Society): Urbanization, social infrastructure. • GS-II (Governance): Welfare programs, housing rights. • GS-III (Economy): Infrastructure, inclusive growth. • Essay: o “Housing is the foundation of dignity and development.” o “Urbanization without affordable housing is a ticking social time bomb.” • PSIR Paper II: State capacity and welfare delivery in developing democracies. ________________________________________

Way Forward
1. Land Reforms: Unlock underutilized government and railway land.
2. Financial Inclusion: Expand interest subvention, micro-mortgages for EWS.
3. Public–Private Partnerships: Incentivize developers to build affordable housing stock.
4. Sustainable Design: Climate-resilient and energy-efficient housing.
5. Rental Housing Policy: Strengthen ARHCs for migrants and students.

Conclusion

Conclusion

India’s urban housing challenge is not just about numbers but about affordability, inclusivity, and resilience. Addressing it requires integrated reforms in land use, finance, and urban planning. For UPSC aspirants, this issue captures how social justice, economy, and governance intersect in building sustainable cities.
