India’s Food Security — Balancing Production, Distribution, and Nutrition

Introduction

Food security has been central to India’s policy since Independence. In 2024, The Hindu highlighted challenges in ensuring availability, access, and nutritional adequacy despite self-sufficiency in grain production. The issue has gained renewed importance as climate change, market volatility, and malnutrition concerns converge. ________________________________________

Introduction

Current Status
• Grain Production: ~330 million tonnes (2023–24), 3rd largest globally.
• Public Distribution System (PDS): Covers ~80 crore beneficiaries under NFSA.
• Food Subsidy Bill: ~₹2.3 lakh crore (2024–25).
• Nutrition Indicators: NFHS-5 (2021) — 35% of children stunted, 19% wasted, 57% anaemic.
________________________________________
Challenges
1. Overdependence on Rice & Wheat: Skewed procurement under MSP regime.
2. Nutrition Deficit: Lack of pulses, millets, fruits in diets.
3. Leakages in PDS: Diversion, ghost beneficiaries.
4. Climate Impact: Erratic monsoons affecting yields.
5. Storage & Distribution Losses: FCI faces wastage and high carrying costs.
________________________________________
Government Initiatives
• National Food Security Act (2013): Legal entitlement to foodgrains.
• PM-GKAY: Extended free foodgrain supply post-COVID.
• POSHAN Abhiyaan (2018): Nutrition-focused intervention.
• International Year of Millets (2023): Promoting nutri-cereals.
• One Nation, One Ration Card (ONORC): Portability of PDS across states.
________________________________________
Global Dimension
• FAO Hunger Index (2023): India ranked 111/125, indicating undernutrition challenges.
• WTO Debates: India defends its PDS subsidies under food security concerns.
• Climate Diplomacy: India advocating for climate-resilient agriculture.
________________________________________
Significance
• Social Justice: Food is a basic right and central to dignity.
• Economic Growth: Malnutrition reduces productivity and GDP.
• Political Stability: PDS and food schemes shape electoral outcomes.
• Sustainability: Overuse of water and fertilizers in rice–wheat belt unsustainable.

UPSC Relevance
• GS-II (Governance): Welfare schemes, PDS reforms.
• GS-III (Economy & Agriculture): Food security, climate resilience.
• Essay:
o “Food security is not only about hunger but about nutrition.”
o “India’s development is incomplete without nutrition security.”
• PSIR Paper II: Welfare state and redistributive justice.
________________________________________
Way Forward
1. Diversify Procurement: Encourage pulses, millets, oilseeds.
2. Strengthen Nutrition Programs: Integrate mid-day meals, POSHAN, PDS.
3. Digitise PDS: Use Aadhaar, GPS tracking, social audits.
4. Climate-Resilient Agriculture: Promote drought-tolerant crops, water efficiency.
5. Public Awareness: Nutrition education at community level.
________________________________________
Conclusion

Conclusion

India’s food security story has shifted from calories to nutrition. While grain sufficiency is achieved, equitable distribution and nutritional adequacy remain challenges. For UPSC aspirants, this issue reflects the intersection of agriculture, welfare, and human development.
