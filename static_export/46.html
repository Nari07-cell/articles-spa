India’s Energy Transition — Balancing Coal and Renewables

The Hindu | Date: 2024 • ~600 words

Introduction

India’s energy transition is one of the most complex in the world. While it has set ambitious renewable energy targets, coal remains the backbone of electricity generation. In 2024, The Hindu examined the dilemma of balancing energy security with climate commitments. This issue is central to UPSC under economy, environment, and governance. ________________________________________ Current Energy Mix (2024) • Coal: ~70% of electricity generation. • Renewables (solar, wind, hydro, biomass): ~30% share, fastest-growing segment. • Targets: o 500 GW non-fossil capacity by 2030. o Net-zero by 2070. • Solar Capacity: ~70 GW installed; wind ~44 GW. ________________________________________ Drivers of Renewable Push 1. Climate Commitments: Paris Agreement, COP28 pledges. 2. Energy Security: Reduce import dependence (oil, coal, gas). 3. Falling Costs: Solar and wind now cheaper than coal in many regions. 4. Technology: Advances in storage, EVs, and green hydrogen. ________________________________________ Challenges 1. Coal Dependence: India still building new coal plants due to rising demand. 2. Grid Integration: Renewable intermittency strains distribution companies (DISCOMs). 3. Financing: Requires $10 trillion till 2070 for net-zero transition. 4. Land Acquisition: Solar/wind parks face local resistance, displacement issues. 5. Just Transition: Coal belt livelihoods (Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Odisha) at risk. ________________________________________ Government Initiatives • National Solar Mission (2010): Pioneering renewable expansion. • Green Hydrogen Mission (2023): $2.4 billion package for electrolyzers, green ammonia. • Perform, Achieve, Trade (PAT): Market-based mechanism for efficiency. • PM-KUSUM: Decentralized solar for farmers. • Renewable Energy Parks: Large-scale installations in Rajasthan, Gujarat. ________________________________________ International Dimension • ISA (International Solar Alliance): India-led, 110+ members. • Quad & G20: Energy transition cooperation. • Foreign Investments: Japan, US, EU funding solar and green hydrogen projects. ________________________________________ UPSC Relevance • GS-III (Environment & Economy): Energy security, climate change. • GS-II (IR): Climate diplomacy. • Essay: o “Energy transition is the bridge between growth and sustainability.” o “Coal versus renewables: India’s development dilemma.” • PSIR Paper II: Global governance and climate justice. ________________________________________

Way Forward
1. Accelerate Renewables: Expand solar rooftops, offshore wind.
2. Grid Modernization: Smart grids, energy storage systems.
3. Phased Coal Transition: Close old, inefficient plants while investing in reskilling workers.
4. Finance Mobilization: Green bonds, international climate finance.
5. Decentralized Energy: Promote local microgrids and rural solar projects.

Conclusion

Conclusion

India’s energy transition reflects a balancing act between climate responsibility and development imperatives. While coal cannot be abandoned immediately, accelerating renewables and ensuring a just transition will determine India’s energy future. For UPSC aspirants, this theme illustrates how environment, economy, and international politics converge in policymaking.
