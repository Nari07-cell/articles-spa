India’s Gati Shakti Plan — Integrating Infrastructure for Growth

Economic Times | Date: 2024 • ~600 words

Introduction

Infrastructure is the backbone of India’s growth aspirations. In 2024, Economic Times highlighted the progress of PM Gati Shakti — National Master Plan for Multimodal Connectivity (2021), which aims to integrate transport, logistics, and digital infrastructure. The initiative seeks to cut logistics costs, boost competitiveness, and enhance ease of living. For UPSC, this reflects how economy, governance, and planning intersect. ________________________________________ What is PM Gati Shakti? • Launch: October 2021. • Objective: Develop a digital platform integrating 16 ministries for coordinated infrastructure planning. • Target: Reduce logistics cost from ~13–14% of GDP to ~8% by 2030. • Approach: Geographic Information System (GIS)-based mapping of existing and planned assets. ________________________________________ Progress So Far (2024) • Projects Onboarded: >1,500 projects mapped across railways, highways, ports, airports, and power. • Integration: Unified planning reduces duplication and delays. • Flagship Projects: o Dedicated Freight Corridors (DFC). o Bharatmala highways. o Sagarmala port-led development. o UDAN regional connectivity. ________________________________________ Significance 1. Logistics Efficiency: Cuts transport time and costs. 2. Competitiveness: Boosts exports by improving supply chains. 3. Job Creation: Large-scale employment in construction and logistics. 4. Regional Balance: Focus on Northeast, border states, and rural connectivity. 5. Digital Integration: GIS mapping for transparency and monitoring. ________________________________________ Challenges 1. Implementation Gaps: Project delays due to land acquisition and clearances. 2. Centre–State Coordination: Varying priorities and execution capacities. 3. Financing Needs: Estimated $1.5 trillion investment needed by 2030. 4. Environmental Concerns: Infrastructure expansion risks ecological damage. 5. Data Accuracy: Reliability of GIS mapping depends on continuous updates. ________________________________________ International Comparison • China: Integrated logistics parks reduced costs to ~7% of GDP. • EU: Trans-European Transport Network (TEN-T) as benchmark for multimodal connectivity. India seeks to replicate such integration while ensuring democratic transparency. ________________________________________ UPSC Relevance • GS-III (Economy & Infrastructure): Logistics, transport, energy. • GS-II (Governance): Inter-ministerial coordination, planning. • Essay: o “Infrastructure is the foundation of economic transformation.” o “India’s growth story is incomplete without logistics reform.” • PSIR Paper II: State capacity in economic planning. ________________________________________
