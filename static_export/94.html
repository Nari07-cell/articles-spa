India’s MSME Sector — Engines of Growth Amid Structural Challenges

The Hindu | Date: 2024 • ~600 words

Introduction

Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) are the backbone of India’s economy, contributing ~30% to GDP and employing over 11 crore people. In 2024, The Hindu discussed how MSMEs remain critical for growth but struggle with credit access, digital adoption, and competitiveness. Introduction

Current Status
• Contribution: 30% of GDP, 48% of exports.
• Employment: 11 crore workers.
• Units Registered (Udyam, 2024): 3.2 crore.
• Credit Gap: Estimated at ₹25 lakh crore.
• Digital Divide: Many small firms lag in e-commerce adoption.
________________________________________
Achievements
1. Job Creation: MSMEs largest employment generator after agriculture.
2. Export Role: Significant contributor to textiles, pharma, handicrafts.
3. Formalisation: Udyam portal improved registration.
4. Cluster Development: State/central schemes boosted competitiveness.
5. Resilience: Quick recovery post-COVID compared to large firms.
________________________________________
Key Challenges
1. Credit Access: Banks reluctant due to high NPAs in MSME lending.
2. Informality: Majority of MSMEs unregistered and unbanked.
3. Technology Gaps: Poor digital adoption in global supply chains.
4. Compliance Burden: Complex GST and labour laws.
5. Global Competition: Cheaper imports undercut small industries.
________________________________________
Government Measures
• Emergency Credit Line Guarantee Scheme (ECLGS, 2020): ₹3.6 lakh crore loans sanctioned.
• MSME Development Act (2006): Defined classification and benefits.
• Udyam Registration (2020): Simplified online registration.
• Digital Initiatives: ONDC for small retailers.
• Cluster Development Programs: Tech centres, export promotion.
________________________________________
Significance
• Inclusive Growth: MSMEs provide livelihoods to women, SC/ST, rural workers.
• Balanced Development: Spread across small towns and rural areas.
• Innovation: Startups and small firms drive entrepreneurship.
• Economic Resilience: Diversifies economy, reduces overdependence on large corporates.
________________________________________
UPSC Relevance
• GS-III (Economy): MSME reforms, employment.
• GS-II (Governance): Welfare for small enterprises.
• Essay:
o “MSMEs are the silent engines of India’s growth.”
o “Empowering small enterprises is empowering India’s democracy.”
• PSIR Paper II: Political economy and inclusive development.
________________________________________
Way Forward
1. Credit Deepening: Expand priority sector lending, fintech solutions.
2. Digitalisation: Training for e-commerce, AI, blockchain in MSMEs.
3. Ease of Doing Business: Simplify GST, compliance burden.
4. Skill Development: Workforce training aligned to MSME needs.
5. Global Integration: Support for exports, trade fairs, quality certification.
________________________________________
Conclusion

Conclusion

MSMEs embody India’s entrepreneurial spirit and resilience, but require structural support to scale globally. Strengthening credit, digital adoption, and competitiveness will help them drive inclusive, job-rich growth. For UPSC aspirants, MSMEs illustrate the convergence of economy, social justice, and governance.
