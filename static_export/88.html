India’s Agrarian Distress — Structural Issues and Policy Responses

The Hindu | Date: 2024 • ~600 words

Introduction

Agriculture sustains nearly 50% of India’s workforce, yet contributes only ~18% of GDP. In 2024, The Hindu highlighted recurring farmer protests, stagnant incomes, and climate-related risks. Introduction

Current Status
• Farm Income Growth: Annual farm income growth <2% (2015–24).
• Landholdings: 86% farmers are small/marginal (<2 hectares).
• Debt Burden: Average farm household debt ~₹74,000 (NSSO, 2022).
• MSP Coverage: Only ~25% farmers benefit from MSP procurement.
• Climate Stress: 2023–24 heatwaves and erratic monsoons reduced yields.
________________________________________
Structural Causes
1. Fragmented Landholdings: Low productivity, mechanisation challenges.
2. Input Costs: Rising fertiliser, diesel, and electricity prices.
3. Market Inefficiencies: APMC monopoly, poor storage, middlemen exploitation.
4. Credit Gaps: Dependence on informal moneylenders.
5. Climate Vulnerability: Droughts, floods, and pest attacks worsening.
________________________________________
Government Measures
• PM-KISAN: ₹6,000 annual income support to farmers.
• PM Fasal Bima Yojana: Crop insurance for risk protection.
• MSP Policy: 23 crops covered, but weak procurement beyond wheat/paddy.
• Soil Health Cards: Promote balanced fertiliser use.
• Agri Infra Fund (2020): ₹1 lakh crore for storage, cold chains.
________________________________________
Achievements
• Direct Support: PM-KISAN covers 11 crore farmers.
• Digital Reforms: e-NAM linking 1,200+ mandis.
• Irrigation Expansion: PM Krishi Sinchai Yojana improving coverage.
• FPO Push: 10,000 Farmer Producer Organisations being promoted.
________________________________________
Persistent Gaps
• Low MSP Penetration: Procurement skewed to Punjab–Haryana.
• Insurance Coverage Issues: Delays, low compensation in PMFBY.
• Storage Deficit: 30–40% horticulture lost post-harvest.
• Policy Fragmentation: State–Centre tensions over farm reforms.
• Income Uncertainty: No assured income for majority of small farmers.
________________________________________
Significance
• Economic Growth: Agriculture key to demand generation in rural India.
• Social Stability: Farmer distress linked to protests and suicides.
• Food Security: Stable farm incomes essential for production.
• Climate Resilience: Sustainable farming critical to adaptation.
________________________________________
UPSC Relevance
• GS-III (Agriculture & Economy): MSP, reforms, subsidies.
• GS-II (Governance): Welfare delivery, Centre–State issues.
• Essay:
o “The farmer feeds the nation but struggles to feed himself.”
o “Agrarian distress is India’s silent crisis.”
• PSIR Paper II: State capacity and rural governance.
________________________________________
Way Forward
1. Crop Diversification: Shift from wheat–paddy to pulses, millets, oilseeds.
2. Market Reforms: Strengthen e-NAM, allow contract farming with safeguards.
3. Income Security: Expand DBT and targeted subsidies.
4. Climate Adaptation: Drought-resistant seeds, micro-irrigation.
5. Institutional Support: Strengthen cooperatives and FPOs.
________________________________________
Conclusion

Conclusion

India’s agrarian crisis is rooted in structural economic and governance gaps. Real solutions lie in income security, diversification, and climate resilience. For UPSC aspirants, it highlights how economy, governance, and social justice converge in rural India.
