India’s Inflation Management — RBI, Fiscal Policy, and Household Impact

Economic Times | Date: 2024 • ~600 words

Introduction

Inflation has long been a sensitive issue in India, influencing not just economics but also politics. In 2024, Economic Times reported how food and fuel prices spiked intermittently, testing the Reserve Bank of India’s (RBI) inflation-targeting framework and government’s fiscal management. Introduction

Current Status
• CPI Inflation (2024 avg): ~5.5%, within RBI’s 2–6% band but volatile.
• Food Inflation: Double digits in pulses and vegetables.
• Fuel Inflation: Moderated due to global oil price corrections.
• RBI Repo Rate: Maintained at 6.5% through most of 2024 after hikes in 2022–23.
• Rupee: Hovered ~₹83/$, cushioning import costs.
________________________________________
Drivers of Inflation
1. Supply-Side Shocks: Weather disruptions, global commodity volatility.
2. Structural Bottlenecks: Pulses, edible oils, and cold storage shortages.
3. Imported Inflation: Geopolitical tensions raising energy prices.
4. Fiscal Stimulus: Welfare schemes and subsidies adding demand pressures.
5. Expectations: Inflation psychology shaping consumption behaviour.
________________________________________
RBI’s Role
• Flexible Inflation Targeting (2016): CPI 4% ± 2%.
• Policy Moves:
o Raised repo by 250 bps in 2022–23.
o Paused hikes in 2024 to support growth.
• Communication: Forward guidance to anchor market expectations.
________________________________________
Government Measures
• Food Management: Import duty cuts on pulses/oil, buffer stock releases.
• Fuel Excise Adjustments: Calibrated excise duty to cushion petrol/diesel prices.
• Targeted Subsidies: LPG, fertilizer, and foodgrain subsidies.
• Infrastructure Push: Cold chains, irrigation, and logistics to reduce supply bottlenecks.
________________________________________
Challenges
1. Volatile Food Prices: Monsoon dependency makes inflation unpredictable.
2. Welfare vs Fiscal Prudence: Balancing subsidies with deficit targets.
3. Global Uncertainty: Oil and geopolitical risks remain high.
4. Rural–Urban Divide: Inflation impacts poorest households most.
5. Political Pressure: Inflation often shapes electoral outcomes.
________________________________________
Significance
• Economic Stability: High inflation erodes investor confidence.
• Household Welfare: Hurts poor most, especially women and children.
• Policy Credibility: RBI’s autonomy tested by political pressures.
• Democracy: Inflation is a political issue with direct voter impact.

Way Forward
1. Diversify Agriculture: Reduce pulses and oilseed dependency on imports.
2. Strengthen Supply Chains: Cold storage, logistics, irrigation.
3. Monetary–Fiscal Coordination: Balance inflation targeting with welfare.
4. Data Transparency: Improve real-time inflation data.
5. Inclusive Focus: Protect vulnerable groups via targeted subsidies.

Conclusion

Conclusion

India’s inflation management reflects the balancing act between growth, welfare, and stability. While RBI and government coordination has contained inflation within targets, structural reforms in agriculture and energy are essential for long-term stability. For UPSC aspirants, this demonstrates how economics and governance intersect in everyday policymaking.
